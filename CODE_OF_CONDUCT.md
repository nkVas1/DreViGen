# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Argue about ideas as hard as you like; do not make it about the person.

## The standard

This project is about families — including families with complicated histories, people who were
persecuted, relationships that were not recorded, and ancestries that are not tidy. Genealogy
touches ethnicity, religion, migration, adoption, illegitimacy and repression as a matter of
routine. That places a particular obligation on how we talk to each other.

**Expected:**

- Disagree with the argument, not the arguer. "This ADR is wrong because X" is welcome. "You are
  an idiot for writing this ADR" is not.
- Assume the other person knows something you do not. In a domain this deep, they usually do.
- Accept correction gracefully, and offer it the same way.
- Respect the privacy of people in the data. Never post a real person's details in an issue,
  a PR or a screenshot. Anonymise fixtures.

**Not tolerated:**

- Harassment, in public or private.
- Discriminatory language or conduct of any kind — including about ethnic or national origin,
  which in a genealogy project can arrive dressed as historical interest.
- Using genealogical data or discussion to make claims about the worth of people or groups.
  The moment ancestry becomes a hierarchy, the conversation is over.
- Publishing anyone's private information without permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussion.

## Scope

Applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code
comments — and when representing the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report to the maintainer through
[GitHub private reporting](https://github.com/nkVas1/DreViGen/security/advisories/new) or by
opening an issue asking for private contact.

Reports are handled confidentially. Responses range from a private word, through a warning, to
a temporary or permanent ban, proportionate to what happened and whether it continued after
being raised.

The maintainer is not exempt from this document.
